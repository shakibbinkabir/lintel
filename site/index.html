Small on purpose.

Lintel is built around four things: efficiency, optimisation, privacy and necessity.

0.5%
It rests when you rest.About half a percent of one processor core while it waits, hidden and idle, in the audit of version 1.0.0. The core is Rust, with a light web view for the interface.
6px
It stays out of your way.A strip six pixels tall is all that waits at the top of your screen. Point at it and the island slides out. Look away and it leaves.
0
Your data stays yours.On a fresh install Lintel calls no server at all. No account, no telemetry. Your keys are kept in Windows Credential Manager, and nothing is approved, sent or deleted without your click.
38
Carry only what you need.Every feature has its own switch. One that is off loads nothing: no hooks, no timers, no threads, no windows.

The Mac has a shelf of notch apps. Windows has very few.

On a Mac you can choose between NotchNook, The Boring Notch, Alcove, DynamicLake, MediaMate, TopNotch, NotchBar, NotchFlow, DropNotch, Coucou and Vorssaint. They are good work, and they showed what a notch can do.

Windows has a handful, such as DynamicWin, Notchify, Venu and Dynamic Edge, mostly for media and widgets. None of them does all of this. So I built Lintel. It grew from the Windows edition of Coucou, brings the best ideas of Vorssaint and Boring Notch to Windows, and adds its own. It is an independent project, and it thanks all three.

It is made for Windows 10 and 11 first. It is not a port waiting for attention. And the name: a lintel is the beam across the top of a door or a window. Lintel sits at the top of your Windows.

Why it exists, and who to thank

A permission card, the Home view with two agent sessions, and the page where every feature has its switch.

All screenshots

One island. Eleven groups of tools.

Everything is a glance away, and each tool can be switched off.

Agents

Sessions, permission cards, plans and questions from your coding agents, on Windows, in WSL and over SSH, with usage and a weekly recap.

Chat

Ask a model from the island, with your own key or a model on your PC, and let it use your MCP tools.

Media

The song that is playing, even across browser tabs, and the volume of each app.

Island

Calendar, reminders, timers, notes and quick controls, one tab each, and notifications from your other apps.

System

Volume and brightness levels, battery, displays, keep awake and a system monitor.

Capture

Screenshots, a richer editor, scrolling capture and screen recording.

Files

A shelf to park files, and a drop on the island to hand a file on.

Tools

Clipboard history, a cleaner with a disk map, a command bar, dictation and a voice assistant.

Apps

Updates and installs for your apps, through winget. Lintel can update itself from the island, when you agree.

Input

Text snippets, and rules for keys, mouse buttons and scrolling.

Windows

Snap windows to halves, thirds and quarters, pin one on top, and move them by dragging.

Built in the open.

Lintel is open source, and everyone is welcome to help: translate it, test it on your PC, fix a bug or build a feature. A feature is one folder, and there is a guide and a short list of rules.
